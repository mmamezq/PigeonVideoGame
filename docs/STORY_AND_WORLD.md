# Story and World

> [!NOTE]
> Draft v2 (2026-11-07). Items marked *(proposed)* are new suggestions that
> still need agreement before they become decisions.

## Story summary

Our hero is the champion of the Modena roost, and he has become arrogant. He
wins on raw strength and stopped practicing the old displays long ago. In a
grand duel, a pompous rival puffs up and coos at him, and each display shakes
him more until he is spooked and loses. By roost tradition, the loser is cast
out.

He wanders the towns of the region alone. In the first town, an old master
helps him relearn the basic displays. On the road he fights other birds, helps
the townsfolk, and learns from masters and opponents what the displays are
really for. At the end he returns to Modena for a rematch against a rival who has been
studying him all along.

## Emotional core

He did not lose because he was weak. He lost because he became arrogant and
stopped paying attention.

The journey is about becoming a better knight and a better bird, not only a
stronger one. This theme drives the mechanics too: combat rewards reading your
opponent, and the rival wins the first duel by reading the hero.

## Story beats

### Opening: the fall (story and tutorial)

- The hero is introduced as the reigning champion, cocky and beloved.
- In the big duel, the rival's displays lower the hero's Attack and Nerve.
- The hero gets spooked and loses.
- He is cast out of the roost.
- The player learns that displays are powerful and that the hero has none.

### Remembering: the first town *(proposed)*

- In the first town after the exile, an old master (perhaps a retired
  champion) drills him through short training fights.
- He relearns basic puff, coo, and turn within roughly the first hour.
- He never truly lost the displays. He got lazy, so they are rusty.
  Relearning them is quick; understanding them takes the rest of the game.

### Understanding: the journey *(proposed)*

- He travels through the towns of the region, fighting and helping townsfolk.
- Each town's master or champion teaches a variation or combo of a display he
  already knows, suited to that town's challenge or local species.
- He also learns by paying attention: studying the displays opponents use on
  him and earning them for himself.
- He grows as a character, not only as a fighter.

### Ending: the rematch

- He returns to Modena for a rematch.
- The rival has studied his fights all along and counters his old habits.
- To win, the hero must fight with everything he has learned, not the way he
  used to. The rival counters basic habits, so the variations and combos
  learned on the road are the key.

Ending options (to decide):

1. He wins and reclaims his place.
2. He wins but chooses to give up the title.
3. He loses but earns the roost's respect.

> [!TIP]
> Options 2 and 3 fit the emotional core best, because both show that the hero
> no longer needs the title.

- A pompous pigeon who still practices every display
- Watches the hero's fights throughout the game
- Open question: pure villain, or someone who has a point? *(Proposed lean:

## World

### Setting

A medieval Emilia-Romagna where animals live in walled towns. Birds settle
disputes through duels and displays. Other animals run the towns but never
fight.

### Lore inspiration *(proposed)*

- Medieval Modena had a real pigeon-flying contest. Pigeon keepers called
  *triganieri* flew their flocks around the city, including near the
  Ghirlandina bell tower, and tried to lure away rival pigeons.
- The Modena roost could sit at the top of the Ghirlandina tower, and its
  duel traditions could grow out of the triganieri contests.

### Animal townsfolk (non-combatants)

Only birds fight, and not every bird fights. Owls and crows are birds who live
as townsfolk and never duel.

| Species | Role                    | Personality                     |
|---------|-------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Cats    | Royalty and court       | Elegant, aloof, a little lazy   |
| Dogs    | Guards                  | Loyal, earnest, rule-followers  |
| Pigs    | Merchants and bankers   | Jolly, shrewd, love a deal      |
| Geese   | Tax collectors, criers  | Loud, bossy                     |
| Mice    | Craftsfolk and bakers   | Hardworking, clever, overlooked |
| Owl     | Scholar and wizard      | Wise, absent-minded             |
| Crows   | Rogues and black market | Sly but fair                    |

Townsfolk give quests, sell items, gossip, and react to the hero's reputation.

### Town structure

Each town is a small isometric hub with:

- Animal townsfolk with quests and dialogue
- A local arena with a ladder of challengers
- Main quests and side quests, some of which involve fights
- Birds you can provoke into fights
- A master or champion who teaches a display technique
